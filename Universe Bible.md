## Star Date System
- The in-universe calendar. Not tied to Earth years, Earth's calendar died with Gerald.
- Format: Star Date [Year]E[Epoch]. Read as "Year [Year] of Epoch [Epoch]." 
- 1 Epoch = 500,000,000 years.
- Current date: Star Date 6526E100. (Year 6526 of Epoch 100.)
- 1 Day = 48 hours. 24 hours wake, 24 hours rest.
- 1 Month = 30 days.
- 1 Year = 48 months (1,440 days / 69,120 hours).
- Conversion: 1 Star Date year ≈ 3.9 Earth years.
- The Mysis-Human treaty was signed approximately 1 Star Date year ago (~3.9 Earth years). Armageddon-G happened during the signing
- The war lasted 10 Earth years prior to the treaty.
- "Years ago" means different things to different species. Potential source of cultural confusion.
- Species existing since the actual beginning of the universe (Epoch 0) means the current date, Epoch 100, places the universe at roughly 50 billion years old

## Planet Naming Convention
- Format: [Planet Name]-[Quadrant]-[Section][Star System Shorthand]
- Star System Shorthand: first two letters of the star system name, inverted. (Tberrius = Tb → tB)
- Section number indicates which section of the star system the planet is in.
- Example: Varaxian-Omegaralis-3tB = Planet Varaxian, Quadrant Omegaralis, Section 3 of Tberrius.
- Example: Mysis-Delatron-5tB = Planet Mysis, Quadrant Delatron, Section 5 of Tberrius.

- Storefronts were vandalized and destroyed in wealthy Oracle districts. Unknown neurological side effects make the wealthy want nothing to do with them.
- Paradoxically, ParasiteHarvester™ does better in the slums. Risk tolerance scales with desperation.
- Only a few Oracles have a ParasiteHarvester depot at all.
- Even in the slums, civilian parasites are out of reach for most people. You'd need to rob a bank.
- Military success with parasites is their best sales pitch. "The soldiers use them."
- A civilian wearing a parasite is genuinely unusual. Enough to turn heads. They either have money, robbed someone, or have a story.
- If you die in a ParasiteHarvester location, that's on you. You sign wrongful death papers and Disclosure agreements.
- They have a body pit. It gets cleaned every week by a Grahgorgaelian.
- ParasiteHarvesters who have men in the company, usually have them on Grahgorgaelian hunts. Capturing them and caging them, and then keeping them hungry so they can eat the pits clean.
- Not the default. They usually scope for Hunters to fill that role. Most men usually get corporate or desk jobs within the corporate buildings, not the ParasiteHarvester depots. (Reason: Brood Mother dens are female-only due to male vulnerability to telepathic takeover, so the core parasite industry is female-fronted; men land in the roles that don't involve dens at all, like Grahgorgaelian hunting.)

## Grahgorgaelians
- A race of alien space animals. Carrion Animals basically.
- They can range from Massive to tiny. They're also a massive threat. They like to cling to faces. Devouring the person outside in.


## Zlarts

- lives in swamp style biomes
- Looks like a cross between a hammerhead shark and a salamander
- The eyes can see to the sides, or stalk together and see in front.
- Probably on planets with extreme swampy biomes. The Mysis planet has some swampy biomes.

## Brood Mothers
- Massive creatures. Incredibly dangerous.
- Fly through space naturally.
- Extremely territorial in whatever space they occupy.
- Around 500 total.
- Some captured. Some still free in space.
- Don't seem to mind captivity as long as the space feels like theirs.
- Dens are caves bored out specifically to house them. They move in and claim the territory.
- Industry is probably corporate.

## Dens
- Gooey, drippy caves. Like a xenomorph egg den. Creepy.
- Purpose built for Brood Mothers.
- You do not want to meet a Brood Mother.

## Brood Mother 
- Brood Mothers are telepathic. They project it, the host doesn't need to have the ability.
- Female beings can communicate with them telepathically.
- Males are vulnerable to takeover and control. Females are at less risk. Biological reason.
- Well documented. Corporate dens are female-only zones. No males allowed.

## Den Operations
- Female handlers enter the den, retrieve birthed parasites, hand off to incubation.
- Incubation → hibernation → shipped to stores or military outposts.
- Locations (stores, outposts) have their own dens.
- Industry is corporate.

## Brood Mother, Captivity
- Fully aware of the arrangement.
- Do not mind. As long as they are not threatened, they are indifferent to being used for profit.
- Not victims. Not tricked. Not broken. Just unbothered.
- No maternal attachment. Produce massive amounts of offspring daily. It's just Tuesday to them.

## Parasites, Development
- Born as infants. Personality and identity develop through bonding with the host.
- Forms are present at birth but can evolve with maturity.
- No two parasites are alike. The species is uniquely individual.
- Parasites are parasites. Unchecked they behave as such, drain, latch, give nothing back.
- Symbiosis only occurs when bonded to a sentient and sapient host. The sapience is what triggers the shift.
- Still hyper intelligent even unbonded. But cannot grow or develop without a host.
- Bonding is in their nature. Unbonded parasites will do their best to be chosen. Like shelter dogs.
- Unbonded lifespan: ~10 years.
- Without bonding, their armor carapace never grows. They stay fleshy. Fragile and easy to kill.
- Require careful handling and care while unbonded.
- Once bonded and mature, the carapace grows around the host. The carapace IS the body armor.
- How much of the host is covered depends on the individual parasite. Entirely unique per parasite.
- Parasites bond to the head because they need to access the central nervous system. Brain to spinal cord is the fastest route.
- Feeding and bonding happen through this connection simultaneously.

## Parasites, Host Bond
- Parasite leeches off host vitals. Links them permanently.
- Parasite augments the host's abilities.
- Bond is both biological and emotional.
- If the host dies, the parasite dies. If the parasite dies, the host dies.
- Removal is possible. Excruciating for both. Both survive.
- Removed parasites are still linked to the host. Killing the unequipped parasite still kills the host.
- You couldn't take off your parasite like that. Well you could, but expect to have SEVERE PAIN and EMOTIONAL TRAUMA afterwards. It's usually only done by surgeons and medical professionals.
- Theoretically you could have a parasite tail lobbed off and survive. You need to destroy the parasites brain. Which is usually located in the head.
- You're not bonding with any other parasite after you remove your first one. Either put it back, which good luck, you pissed it off already, unless this was something you both already agreed to, it's not going to be too happy with the fact you already removed it once.

## Parasites, Bonding Process
- When you get a Parasite either bought and pattern matched, or thrust to you by the Military, you'll be guided to an empty room. Inside of it will be you, the parasite and a locked door. They'll throw you in there, lock the door, and the Parasite will attack you and latch onto your skull, slightly crushing it. Then the most painful process of your life begins as the Parasite penetrates the base of the neck and fuses to your spinal cord.
- 0.5% failure rate. If you don't survive the process, the Parasite won't bond, but eat you.
- The bonding itself determines the link, if the bonding fails, a link was never made.
- The 0.5% failure rate should be taken with a grain of salt. Frail people are the ones who have that 0.5% failure rate. You're advisedly warned off if you're elderly or frail, but if you pay, they're not gonna stop you. The Military will kick you and disbar you if you're not applicable for a Parasite.

## Parasites, Communication
- All parasites are brain linked to their host regardless of gender. Information flows both ways.
- Female hosts: telepathic communication is clear. Abstract thought on both sides until maturity, sharpens over time.
- Male hosts: brain link exists but the information is harder to interpret. Signal is there, clarity isn't.
- Parasites bonded to male hosts most likely evolve tentacles. Used for a sign language communicated to their host.
- Female hosts may also learn sign language with their parasite for stealth purposes.
- Parasites can hear regardless of host gender.
- Some parasites evolve the ability to speak. Rare. Requires a second mouth orifice, the primary mouth is occupied bonding to the host's skull.
- A parasite that evolves speech can communicate verbally regardless of host gender.

## Parasites, Augmentation
- Augments vary per parasite. No two are alike.
- Can include: increased strength, speed, flight, and a plethora of other possibilities.
- Gene editing available. Allows selection of specific augments.
- Parasites evolve rapidly.

## Ships
- Inspiration: Sigma Star Saga.
- Brood Mother DNA gene spliced with metal elements. Creates living metal.
- A Brood Mother variant. The ship itself is a living creature.
- You can just use the shells of a parasite egg, or even the sweat of a brood mother itself. All you need to do is separate the DNA from the debris and you can infuse metal and create as much Living Metal as you need to create an entire massive spaceship.
- The computer of the ship is infused with DNA to the point that it stops running on electrical components and works on cells and living matter. That's why it's able to connect to a Parasite.
- Your parasite is able to grab a telekinetic link to the ship and control it. The ships without a pilot are basically scared animals. Flying through space summoning either their dedicated pilot or the nearest one to fight off whatever spooks it.
- Ships will develop faces and what not. Full body horror with the ships, the inside of it fleshy and metallic as well. The outside gives it a face. Terrifying horrific faces with translucent eyes. Something that can be seen through. There is a cockpit, and you can see through it. But the ship also can see itself.
- People are very protective of their ships. They take care of them, feed them, make sure that they're respected. The ships have a tendency to ignore their pilots if you mistreat them.
- The ships when they explode release a death cry. Pitch shifted gutterals.
- Gene splicing removed thought and personality as a side effect. Not intentional. Left only the ability to follow directives.
- It's basically the side effect of the gene-splicing technique used. It wasn't on purpose. It's just when you splice in foreign molecular material with DNA, you end up having to leave a few things on the cutting room floor.
- Look like conventional spacecraft. The Parasite brain replaces what would be a CPU.
- Ships have weapons, lasers and conventional armaments grafted onto the hull. Dumb hardware. Not connected to the ship's brain. The ship cannot fire them alone.
- Ships fly designated routes around a star base.
- When a fleet is scrambled over a planet, any bonded soldier with open sky above them can be beamed up at any moment. Mid-meal, mid-sleep, mid-anything.
- First few times being summoned: weird and fuzzy. You acclimate.
- Soldiers on Star Bases are unlikely to be summoned. Soldiers planetside with a fleet overhead are permanently on call.
- Ships summon any available bonded soldier, not a specific pilot. Weapon configuration is never the same per engagement.
- Weapons are parasite-host linked. The ship contains them, but how they fire and what they fire depends entirely on the bonded soldier beamed up.
- Think RPG progression, the stronger the soldier and parasite bond, the more powerful the ship they inhabit becomes.
- Veteran soldiers are extremely valuable. They can make any ship dangerous at a moment's notice.
- Scrambled fleets are never consistent. Every engagement is a dice roll on available soldiers and their parasite strength.
